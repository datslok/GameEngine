# Build log: Phase 18: A jungle valley showcase (Nov 13 2026 onwards) (part 2 of 2)

## Phase 18: A jungle valley showcase (Nov 13 2026 onwards) (part 2 of 2)
- `tree-growth` (Nov 14 2026): the forest item's first stage, a dozen jungle emergents round the clearing. Explained the choices before building; the user: "sounds good, as you recommend".
  - **Space colonisation over grammars:** Weber-Penn's parametric trees (Blender's Sapling) or L-systems describe a branch by rules; space colonisation (Runions et al. 2007) grows branches towards the room left in a crown's envelope, so they compete for space and fill it without crossing, and the crown's shape is the only thing to choose. An umbrella crown, an oblate ellipsoid 28 m across and 10 m deep, 32 m up, is the jungle emergent's.
  - **A flat skeleton:** nodes in a vector, each naming its parent, parents first; the radii by Leonardo's rule (area kept, n = 2) are then one pass backwards, and the trunk's thickness follows from the twigs (2 cm times the square root of the tips).
  - **A grid for the nearest node:** each round every attractor asks for its nearest node; a grid of cells as wide as the influence (4 m) makes that 27 cells' worth instead of every node.
  - **A guard against repeats:** two attractors pulling a node from opposite sides can leave both nearer the parent than its new child, and the parent would grow the same child forever; no node is grown within a tenth of a step of another.
  - **Buttresses as lobes:** five fins as narrow as cos^24 standing out up to 1.5 trunk radii at the ground and fading by e every 1.5 m up, on a 64-sided trunk, its normals from the surface's own differences; `appendTube` grew options for this and for whole bark tiles round a tube (a 1.5 m trunk's bark stretched over 5 m otherwise), and the ivy and the trees share one leaf card function.
  - **Crown normals:** each leaf card faces out of the crown, its normals bent 60% further towards the crown's own outward direction, so thousands of cards shade as one rounded canopy, lit on the sun's side.
  - **Sight lines** (the user's requirement): from the path every 10 m, the meadow and the clearing to the Monolith's face and the Ring's top; a crown meeting one is not planted. `segmentMeetsSphere` moved from the glass code to `math/closest_points` to serve both.
  - **Tests shared:** the forest tests' copies of a settings field counter and a triangle facing check went into `tests/forest_helpers.h`.
  - **The user's look:** "idk they dont look that good, i was thinking a lush jungle like crysis". Seen from a hidden copy: see-through crowns of scattered pale leaves on thin straight poles, a dozen trees standing alone on bare ground. Explained that a jungle's look is density and layers, and re-planned the forest item (ROADMAP): one patch of real jungle first, every layer in it.
  - **Denser, darker crowns:** leaves along the twigs' last stretches (every node up to `leafyRadius`), not only the tips; the leaf atlas tinted darker (`forestTreeLeafTint`); trunks leaning (a curve, straight at the foot) and tapering. Better, but still an umbrella on a pole.
  - **The research** (the user: "maybe do some research on how to build good looking trees in games?", with a cartoon forest and a photo of a mossy temperate rainforest): `docs/TREES.md`. Apical control decides pole or fork (Palubicki et al. 2009); crowns are clumps with gaps; Leonardo's n is 2-3; tropism makes limbs crooked; games put many leaves on each card. The user then made it a rule: research anything new before starting (CLAUDE.md).
  - **The tree's shape from the research:** the trunk forks at 9 m into 3-5 limbs kinked by noise, each walking to the middle of its own clumps (9 spheres of about 3.5 m in the crown's outer part, shared among the limbs by direction); space colonisation from the scaffold into attractors inside the clumps only, with a gravity pull of 0.2; Leonardo's n 2.5. One even envelope gave an even lid; clumps give gaps and depth. The user: "thats much better".
  - **Tests' time:** every forest test built the valley with four full trees (2500 attractors each); `TestForest` now grows two sparse ones (300 attractors). The suite took 297 s with the full trees and 226 s after; the rest of the climb from about 100 s (Oct 29) was not traced (it was the golden views test, below).
  - **Leaf clusters** (docs/TREES.md Part 2): no CC0 cluster texture exists, so one is composed at start-up: twigs of 12-18 `LeafSet024` leaves, each turned with its normal map turned too, in a 2 x 2 atlas; cards only on each clump's shell, fillers inside, normals blended from the card, its clump and the crown. The user chose 30-40 cm leaves over real 15-25 cm ones (bolder, like the broad leaves planned for the understorey). The first version still looked thin from a hidden copy's screenshots; denser defaults (cards of 2 m, two a node, the shell from 0.3 of the clump's radius out, eight fillers, twelve clumps of 4.5 m, 4000 attractors) gave rounded crowns. The user: "looks great, frames are getting slow tho".
  - **A start among the trees** (the user: "can you set the spawn point for the player in the middle of the trees"): a setting rather than a new default, since the rim's start is the valley's design (`forest.valley.start = Trees`, in the user's settings.cfg). First on the path, where no tree stood near: the sight lines keep crowns back from it. Then 12 m south of the tree with the most neighbours.
  - **The reflection probe spread over frames:** measured at the user's view (the RTX 4070 Laptop, DLAA, medians of five): the trees cost 2.4 ms (16.7 against 14.3), half of it the probe redrawing them six times a frame, and the probe cost 2.4 ms without any trees. Explained leaving foliage out of the probe or drawing one face a frame; the user chose the second. One face a frame, each cycle from where it started, the outputs made after its sixth face: the capture 3.7 ms to 0.7 ms, the frame 16.8 ms to 14.1 ms.
- `far-shadow-movers` (Nov 14 2026): "the shadows for some of the floating islands are too slow and visibly stutter", exactly twice a second: the drifting rocks' far shadows, the far map kept half a second while things only moved. Researched how engines keep still shadows cached and movers smooth (`docs/SHADOWCACHE.md`: Insomniac's and Unity's copy-then-draw, Unreal's two copies per page, CryEngine, Cyberpunk); explained copying the still picture back and drawing movers over it, against two maps every reader samples; the user chose the first. The user also asked what virtual shadow maps are: explained, and their roadmap item (phase 14) given the forest's reasons.
  - **Moving means its matrix changed since last frame,** which every draw carries already (for motion vectors); the far tile's fingerprint covers its still casters only, so moving rocks no longer redraw it.
  - **A still copy and patches:** a depth image the tile's size holds the still casters; each frame the movers' patches, this frame's and last frame's, are copied back from it (one copy, a region each) and the movers drawn over them. Every reader samples one tile, as before; no shader changed.
  - **The half-second patience removed** (`farShadowRefreshSeconds`, the layout fingerprint and the renderer's shadow clock), as the movers no longer need it.
  - **Merged** with `tree-growth` (the user: "you can merge both").
- `jungle-canopy` (Nov 14 2026): the jungle patch's first stage. The user doubted the trees were 30 m tall; measured on the grown meshes, they were (27.6-30.8 m, scaled 0.85-1.15), and read small from oversized leaves and nothing in between. Researched rainforest layers, sizes, densities and how games draw jungle (`docs/JUNGLE.md`); the user chose the plan and real-sized leaves.
  - **Canopy trees** to 38 m (28 of them, crowns 32 m across), **emergents** of 55 m with a clear trunk to 36 m (5, placed first, the canopy trees kept out from under their crowns), **leaves** of 22 cm on 1 m cards.
  - **Shadows while turning:** the user's overlay showed 40.8 ms frames turning (shadows 23.1 ms) and 18.5 ms still (0.1 ms): the sun's cascades follow the view, so turning redrew 400 thousand leaf cards. Options put to the user (shadow proxies, cascades round the camera, fewer cards, staggered cascades); the user chose proxies: `ModelRenderer::shadowOnly` and shadow leaves with a sixth of the cards. Turning then: 25.9 ms frames, about 7 ms of shadows. Tuning the proxy thinner was put aside (the user: it would make the shadows less real); cheaper far cascades next (`docs/SHADOWCACHE.md`, last part).
  - **Ray tracing:** the shadow leaves are hidden from rays, so with tracing on the leaves cast no traced shadow; splitting `castsShadows` into maps and rays went to phase 17.
- `understorey-shadow-cascades` (Nov 15 2026): the shadows while turning, 21.9 ms, measured step by step in a hidden copy (the RTX 4070 Laptop).
  - **The understorey in the nearest two cascades** (`ModelRenderer::shadowCascades`, Unity's "Max Shadow Cascade", `docs/SHADOWCACHE.md`): 21.9 to 19.3 ms. Not the main cost.
  - **Shadow bark** at 10-12% of the triangles (bark was nine tenths of the triangles in the cascades): 18.2 ms. Not the main cost either.
  - **The pixels:** cascades of 2048 instead of 4096 cut the shadows from 17.2 to 6.8 ms, so the cost was the cut-out leaf cards' pixels. Measured the composed leaf texture: the twigs cover 14-20% of their cards; an 8-sided outline round them 28-30% (16 sides only 1-2% better). Cards drawn as their cell's outline: 6.9 ms, the turning frame 31.9 ms; the picture unchanged.
- `frame-build-speed` (Nov 15 2026): the user: "now lets fix the time". Measured first in a benchmark of the real forest (21,500 objects): building the frame's draws 25.6 ms (the motion history's two hash maps 14 of it), packing instances and materials 10.8 ms, everything else 1.5 ms. Researched how engines keep many static objects cheap (Unreal's GPU Scene uploading only changed primitives, Unity's BatchRendererGroup with persistent instance data); explained quick wins first and a persistent scene later; the user's go.
  - **Motion history** as flat records by entity slot stamped with their frame; **the draws** built on every core in a fixed order; **materials and instances** packed on every core, indices handed out in order with a small cache of recent materials (draws alternate bark and leaves, so "same as the last draw" would rarely hit).
  - **Measured:** the benchmark's building 25.6 to 3.5 ms and packing 10.8 to 4.6 ms (2.6 of it the work, the rest allocating the frame's buffers); in the game, SCENE 30 to 4.2 ms, the frame 50 to 30.6 ms standing still, now waiting on the GPU (47.8 ms turning, shadows 21.9 ms).

- `cascade-stagger` (Nov 14 2026): the jungle's shadows still cost 7 ms turning after the shadow leaves. The user turned down thinner shadow leaves ("wont f5 tuning just lead to a less realistic shadow landscape") and chose cheaper far cascades. Researched (`docs/SHADOWCACHE.md`, last part): Unreal redraws far cascades less often and can scroll cached ones; Crysis and Unity's instanced renderers give far cascades cheaper meshes; alternate-frame updates can make the frame rate uneven.
  - **How:** the far two cascades take turns, one redrawn a frame, the other keeping its last box (`holdStaggeredCascades`), so every frame does the same work.
  - **Measured** in a hidden copy turning 3 degrees a frame among the trees (the RTX 4070 Laptop): shadows 7.1 to 4.6 ms, frames 25.2 to 22.6 ms; still, 18.3 ms (the main pass 8 ms, DLAA 3.8 ms). The per-cascade GPU timing first offered was dropped: the shadow pass is timed whole, and stagger on against off shows the gain. Coarser leaves for the far cascades wait.
  - **Tests:** a first run failed on the test's own choice of which cascade a frame keeps.
- `distant-occluders` (Nov 14 2026): the islands, 400-700 m up, shadowed the Monolith looking towards the sun but not looking at it. The cascades take casters only 200 m towards the sun (`directionalCasterReach`); looking towards the sun the Monolith was read from the far map, which holds the islands. A reach of 2000 m fixed it but drew the islands into every cascade: shadows from about 2 to 6 ms on the user's overlay, rejected.
  - **Research** (`docs/SHADOWCACHE.md`, second half): the usual fix is no limit towards the sun with casters clamped onto the near plane, our 2000 m's cost, and it breaks blocker-distance soft shadows; Unreal's far cascades and distance field shadows take over only past the near range. No source reads a coarse map's distant blockers inside the near cascades: our own design from known parts (two maps, the darker kept).
  - **How:** inside the cascades, a read of the far map from the point moved the reach towards the sun (only its depth changes there), so only blockers further than the reach count, never the far map's coarse self-shadowing; the darker of it and the cascade's shadow, not the product, since a caster can be in both. The main pass, the fog and the reflection probe's capture; skipped where the cascade's shadow is already full. A test checks that a point any cascade holds, moved the reach, is still in its box, so nothing falls between.
  - **Soft edges:** the user's first look: "that works but the shadows are very low res for things that are far away": one hardware-filtered comparison showed the far map's 2.7 m texels as steps. The tap now takes the far tile's soft shadow (blocker search, penumbra, 16 turned samples), its penumbra from the real distance below the blocker (the reach added back, `softShadowFromTileLifted`); the fog keeps one comparison.
  - **The user's look:** "thats good enough for now". Finer far shadows would need a bigger far map or phase 14's virtual shadow maps.
