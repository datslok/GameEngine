# Build log: Phase 16: Beautiful without ray tracing (Nov 6-13 2026) (part 3 of 3)

## Phase 16: Beautiful without ray tracing (Nov 6-13 2026) (part 3 of 3)
- `frosted-glass-smooth` (Nov 13 2026): the user found the light lab's frosted pane showing the mirror ball's sun glint as a hard-edged bright square, with a whitish patch ending in a straight line. **Diagnosed in their running game** (bloom off: unchanged; refraction off: the pane gone, the glint a tiny spot thousands of times the towers' brightness): a coarse pyramid level holds the glint as one texel up to 64 pixels wide, read bilinearly a tent so bright it stays above white over almost all of it; and each glass pixel's level came from the distance right behind it, so where the background jumped from a far tower to the floor plate the glow stopped in a line. **Explained, then the user chose** both fixes (A and B) over clamping bright light first (which would throw away the glow's real energy).
  - **How:** each whole level read through a cubic B-spline from four bilinear reads (`bsplineAxis`), blended between whole levels, level 0 as before; the level from the distance averaged over the blur's area (the pyramid now averages alpha).
  - **A test that was wrong:** the first version asserted the B-spline is round (diagonal like axis within a tenth); worked out, it is within 6% one texel out but 37% at 1.5 texels, so the test now asserts what matters for hard edges, no kinks (the slope continuous at texel centres, where bilinear filtering's jumps).
  - **What is left:** in a hidden debug copy at the user's pose the square is gone and the glow soft, but the glow still ends along the ball's outline: gathering at each glass pixel's own background distance is not how frosted glass spreads light (each source by its own distance). Noted on the roadmap, to be done with the depth of field's gather.
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: the B-spline equal to its explicit sum, weights adding to one, a lone texel's values and its smoothness); the debug, release and GPU test programs build without warnings; the hidden debug copy reported no validation messages. **Not run:** `make gputest` and the golden views (the light lab's views with frosted glass will differ).

- `specular-anti-aliasing` (Nov 13 2026, on `frosted-glass-smooth`): the user then found that very small camera movements changed the frosted pane's glow drastically. **Measured** in a hidden copy at the pane: 2 mm steps swung its fully white pixels from 5,723 to 11,889. **Cause:** the mirror ball's sun highlight at the smallest roughness is a lobe about 0.002 radians wide, about a millimetre on the ball, smaller than a pixel, so the light pixel centres catch from it swings with every tiny move and TAA jitter; seen directly TAA averages it, but frosted glass reads the frame before anti-aliasing and spreads it over the pane. **Explained, then the user said go:** geometric specular anti-aliasing (Tokuyoshi and Kaplanyan 2019): the roughness widened by the smooth normal's screen-space derivatives, so a pixel shows the highlight averaged over the normals it covers.
  - **A test that was wrong:** the first version asserted a swing under 10% at the paper's variance; worked out in Python it is 17% (3.6 times raw, 1.04 at a variance of 0.5), so the variance became a setting (`specularAntiAliasingVariance`, the paper's 0.25 by default) and the test asserts each figure. In the game the default already holds the pane's glow within 1.2%.
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: flat surfaces unchanged, the widening and its cap, a row of pixels across a mirror ball steady as it slides); the debug, release and GPU test programs build without warnings; the hidden release copy with validation on reported nothing. **Not run:** `make gputest` and the golden views (shiny curved objects' highlights change slightly).
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: the passes' order and names, and four fake stamps' parts adding up to their span); the release and debug games build without warnings. **Not run:** `make gputest` and the golden views (only on request).

- `depth-of-field` (Nov 13 2026): the camera finish's third part, off by default (the user dislikes it). **Explained, then the user said go:** a thin lens's circle of confusion with the focal length from the field of view and a 24 mm sensor, autofocus on the crosshair, and Jimenez 2014's scatter-as-gather at half resolution with near and far layers, its gather meant to serve frosted glass next.
  - **How:** six passes after the anti-aliasing and before motion blur: the focus (on the GPU, eased in dioptres, since the circle of confusion is linear in inverse distance), prepare with the tiles' near reach, their neighbourhoods, the gather (81 samples on four rings), a 3 x 3 median, the composite at full resolution.
  - **A layout changed before building:** with rings at k / R of the reach, a sample on the reach's rim is only half covered by a disc as wide as the reach, so the inside of a blurred foreground came out about 80% opaque. The rings now sit in the middle of their annuli, the gather reaches half a pixel past the largest radius, each sample is weighed by its share of the disc's area, and coverage is a pixel's share of a straight edge (all half a pixel inside, none half a pixel out).
  - **A game's lens blurs little:** at the default f/2.8 and the light lab's field of view, focused on the horizon, nothing blurs past half a blur pixel: the physics, since a wide lens has a deep focus. A hidden copy at f/0.7 focused at 12 m blurred the near pillar and ground (blue with `showFocus`) and barely the far field, as the formula says.
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: the settings, the lens, the easing, coverage, rings, weights, median, prepare, composite, the gathers without a halo and with a spreading foreground, the packing, DOF's place in the timings); the debug, release and GPU test programs build without warnings; a hidden release copy with validation on reported nothing, DOF 0.23 ms at 1920 x 1080 on the laptop. **Not run:** `make gputest` and the golden views (only on request; the default picture is unchanged).

- `frosted-gather` (Nov 13 2026): the frosted pane's glow still ended along the mirror ball's outline after `frosted-glass-smooth`, since each glass pixel blurred by its own background's distance, a gather where the light really scatters. **Explained, then the user said go:** the depth of field's scatter-as-gather on the pane.
  - **How:** a point behind frosted glass spreads over a disc whose radius grows with its distance behind, up to a ceiling for the infinitely far, so every pane pixel knows the largest reach it needs without tiles. The old read by the pixel's own distance became the centre of a gather on the depth of field's rings out to that ceiling, each sample at the level whose texels match the rings' spacing, with its own distance and the near layer's scatter weight (coverage times light over the disc's area, now one shared `scatterWeight`).
  - **A test that was wrong:** the first version put the glint in a 2-pixel stripe sampled at full resolution, which the rings alias (the glow jumped between 30 and 200 from pixel to pixel in a Python check); the shader reads a level whose texels average 4 x 4 pixels, so the test's stripe is now two of those texels wide, and the glow rises steadily towards it and carries across the outline (about 80% of the background's beside it).
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: the disc's radius and ceiling, the level, the shared weight, a uniform background unchanged, a near thing sharp, a far glint's glow crossing its outline); the debug, release and GPU test programs build without warnings; a hidden release copy with validation on reported nothing, and close up the pane's glow round the glint is a soft disc carrying over the ball. GLASS 0.62 ms at 1080p on the laptop with the pane filling much of the screen. **Not run:** `make gputest` and the golden views (frosted glass changes them).

- `image-lights` (Nov 13 2026): the user found the flashlight shone into the light lab's mirror lit nothing it points at, with ray tracing off (the default since phase 16). **Explained, then the user said go:** the image light, moved here from 6e, built now because the user met it in play.
  - **How:** each light in front of a flat smooth metal face gets a spotlight at its mirror image with the face as its window; the source's own shadow at the point of reflection covers the way to the mirror, and the image's own tile, clipped at the glass, the way from it. Being a spotlight, the clusters, captures and fog took it at once.
  - **Also:** the light lab's flashlight became a setting (`lightLab.flashlight.on`, F flips it), the command-line rule's, which also let a hidden copy check this; the "Camera finish" line ticked (its parts were all done).
  - **A test that was wrong:** the first version expected no image for a lamp behind the mirror; a thin metal box is a mirror on both sides, so it rightly lights the far side through the back face, and the test now checks that.
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first); the debug, release and GPU test programs build without warnings (a brace-initialised `PlacedSpotLight` warned of the new field until it got a default); a hidden release copy with validation on reported nothing, and in the mirror's picture the room behind the camera is lit by the reflected beam, dark with `imageLights` off. **Not run:** `make gputest` and the golden views.

- `coloured-shadow-maps` (Nov 13 2026): glass tinting the shadowed lights without ray tracing, the fallback moved here from 6e. **Explained, then the user chose** half size (over full size, about 384 MB more on the laptop's 8 GB) and the light lab's stained glass to judge it by.
  - **How:** beside the shadow atlas, with the same tiles at half size, a tint (the product of every glass face's share each light's view sees, multiply-blended) and the nearest glass depth (max-blended, reversed-Z). A point takes a texel's tint only behind its glass; every comparison of the shadow filters is tinted at its own place, and the blocker search counts the glass, so the tint's edge is as soft as a shadow's at that distance. Glass hidden behind something solid seen from the light is thrown away against the atlas's depth (a basement window must not colour the floor above it). Cached with the tiles: a tile's signature takes its glass in, and is exactly the old one without glass. The fog's grid reads it too.
  - **The memory estimate was wrong:** the plan said about 96 MB, counting 4 bytes a texel; the glass depth needs its own channel (a 16-bit float, whose relative precision suits reversed-Z), so it is 6 bytes: about 150 MB at the default 4096 cascades. The maps are made only once glass is in the world. Two targets blending differently need `independentBlend`, enabled when present (every desktop GPU); without it the maps stay off.
  - **Glass tints whatever its `castsShadows` says,** as the rays already did, so both paths agree; the light lab's panes had it off (from when glass could not cast shadow maps), so its window, glass ball and frosted panel now tint the sun by default. One function gives a face's share for both paths (`glassFaceTransmittance`).
  - **The stained glass** (`lightLab.stainedGlass.on`) swaps the sunlit room's window for 4 x 4 coloured cells in lead. In a hidden debug copy with validation on (no messages) the floor took the cells' colours and the mist coloured shafts. The lead lines blur away on the floor, which is right: the sun's disc smears a 2 cm line over about 2 m. The glass ball's shadow is faint with a darker rim (Fresnel at grazing angles); real glass would also focus the sun into a bright spot, which no shadow map does.
  - **A green glass bottle on the pen table** (the user's ask, after a first look): a new `Mesh::lathe` (a profile spun round an axis, closed by caps), thin green glass, double-sided so the light is tinted by both walls as through a real bottle. A hidden release copy showed its deep green shadow on the table top in sunlight, and a second, lighter one with the desk lamp on.
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: the lathe mesh's rings, winding, outward normals, closed surface and a cone's tip; the bottle on the table clear of the pens; a face's share, behind the glass only, hidden glass, when the maps draw, the half-size map and its squares, each tile's light place and glass flag, the glass in reach, the signature, the shadow view, the stained glass's images and switch); the debug, release and GPU test programs build without warnings. **Not run:** `make gputest` and the golden views (the light lab's views with sun through glass change).

- `clear-coat` (Nov 13 2026): the first of phase 16's materials, a varnish layer over any material (car paint, lacquered wood, varnished floors), as glTF's `KHR_materials_clearcoat` describes it. **Explained, then the user said go**, including the two choices below.
  - **How:** a second GGX lobe, a 4% reflector at its own roughness on the geometry's normal (or its own normal map), times its amount; the base's diffuse and specular parts dimmed by the coat's albedo under light from everywhere, read from the existing specular albedo table, so what the coat reflects is exactly what the base does not get and the white furnace test still holds. glTF dims by Fresnel at the view's angle instead, slightly off for rough coats. Every light goes through `addLight`, so the sun, lamps, mirrored light and the reflection probe's capture all got it at once; the coat's textures are read only on coated materials, so uncoated pixels cost the same.
  - **Reflections follow one layer, the smoother** (the coat on a tie): the reflection pass follows one reflection a pixel, and following both would double its cost. The other layer keeps its stand-in, the probe's or the sky's light.
  - **The light lab** got two red painted balls on the grass between the start and the reflection corner, the same paint, one bare and one lacquered (smooth coat, roughness 0.03).
  - **Switch:** `engine.render.materials.clearCoat` (F5 Materials, on), for comparing.
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: no coat changes nothing to the bit, the coated white furnace, the coat's share rising towards grazing and its lobe returning exactly its albedo, the coat ignoring the normal map and lit from in front of either layer, which layer the reflections follow, packing, loading, the asset manager, the switch by command, the balls' placement); the debug game and GPU test program build without warnings. **Not run:** the game (the look is the user's to judge), `make gputest` (the new `GPU_TEST_CLEAR_COAT` case is built) and the golden views (the light lab's views that see the balls change).

- `parallax-occlusion` (Nov 13 2026): the second of phase 16's materials. **Tessellation was asked about first and dropped** (the user's choice): it is still in every GPU, but tiny triangles waste the 2 x 2 pixel quads GPUs shade in, every pass (the prepass, each shadow tile, the probe's faces) must tessellate identically, and rays never see it; engines moved to parallax for surface detail and to cluster-based geometry (Nanite) for real detail. **Explained, then the user said go**, with self-shadowing and the brick wall.
  - **How:** each pixel's view ray stepped down through the height map (8 steps straight down to 32 at grazing views), the crossing refined between the last two steps (Tatarchuk 2006), every texture then read there with the unshifted footprint's mips; faded out under two pixels of depth. The sun's self-shadow is the same walk up towards it, each sample darkening by how far a bump pokes above the ray over how far it has climbed.
  - **Layout:** the height map's slot and depth share the clear coat's record (`coatAndHeight`), so the material stays 144 bytes; the switches moved into `MaterialSettings` (the clear coat's key unchanged); the frame block grew to 416 bytes.
  - **The light lab** got a low brick wall beside the painted balls and a cobbled strip in front of it, their colour, height and normal maps made in code (the normal map worked out from the height map, `core/height_map`, so the two agree); `tiledPanel` now makes the chain-link fence's panel too.
  - **A test that was wrong:** the normal map's ramp along v was asserted to lean past 0.1; its slope is 0.04, as along u, so it now checks the exact lean.
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: the frame, steps, shift, crossing, a flat map, a tilted plane's exact crossing, a brick's edge hiding the mortar from one side, self-shadowing, the fade, the settings by command, packing, the asset manager, the normal map from heights, the panel, pictures and wall); the debug and release games and the GPU test program build without warnings. **Not run:** the game (the look is the user's to judge), `make gputest` (new `parallax_gpu_test` built) and the golden views (the light lab's views that see the wall change).

- `typical-gpu` (Nov 13 2026): the last item phase 16's milestone hangs on, so features can be judged as most players will see them. **Explained, then the user chose** DLSS at Quality (not FSR) for it, and for the switch only: the presets keep meaning quality, not resolution, and the engine's default stays Ultra with DLAA.
  - **How:** `applyTypicalGpu` sets the High preset, ray tracing off and the upscaler at Quality (DLSS, else FSR, else TAA, by what the PC can run); `TypicalGpuView` keeps the settings it found and gives them back when turned off. F5's checkbox, `typical-gpu on|off` and `--typical-gpu`.
  - **A gap closed:** the quality presets had no command (the command-line rule's); `preset [name]` now shows or sets one.
  - **Ray-traced settings tagged once:** `SettingsVisitor::needingRayQueries` wraps ray tracing and its debug view, ray-traced reflections, the mirrored lights' search and every light probe setting in their `visit()`; F5 greys them on a GPU without ray queries and `set` says they do nothing there.
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: the upscaler's fallback, exactly its settings set, the switch restoring after changes and on twice, `preset` and `typical-gpu` by command, every tagged key and no other, the "no effect" note, the launch option); the debug and release games build without warnings. **Not run:** the game (the milestone check is the user's to make with it on).

- **Phase 16 closed** (Nov 13 2026, `close-phase-16`): the user checked the milestone with the typical GPU setup on, the light lab and the demo by day and at night: "it all looks great". The leftovers moved on: the sky grid's lamps (stage 3), anisotropy and skin to phase 14, sheen to phase 10 (garments), decals to phase 12 (the FPS). **The fog's history in seconds was dropped**, after working it through with the user: a fixed share a frame averages the same 10 frames at any rate, so its noise is steady and only its lag grows at low frame rates (0.32 s at 30 fps, 0.08 at 120); a time constant in seconds keeps the lag but averages only about 3 frames at 30 fps, swapping a slight trail for shimmer, the worse of the two.
