# Build log: Phase 16: Beautiful without ray tracing (Nov 6-13 2026) (part 1 of 3)

## Phase 16: Beautiful without ray tracing (Nov 6-13 2026) (part 1 of 3)

- `replan-beauty-first` (Nov 6 2026): the plan changed. The user: "lets put DDGI on hold for now, build all the features we need for non rtx gpus to look beautiful, and then we make ray tracing and path tracing".
  - **Why now:** since `ray-tracing-off` the default path has no bounce light at all, only the flat ambient, and most players are on that path.
  - **Asked first; the user chose** screen-space GI for bounce light, over the software tracer (it restarts probe work, and is large) and baked lighting (it needs a bake tool and cannot follow a moving sun). The accepted cost: closed rooms stay lit by the flat ambient.
  - **Cloth parked, not merged:** the user chose to leave `gpu-self-collision` unmerged and rebuild self-collision later with a more real-time friendly system. At that point the pile's local pinches were still open and the lab ran about 45 ticks a second with self-collision on.
  - **DDGI off by its own switch too:** `GlobalIlluminationSettings::enabled` now defaults to off (the user: "turn off ddgi by default"), so turning ray tracing on no longer brings the probes back, and the screen-space bounce light keeps running.
  - **Numbering:** the new phases are 16 and 17, placed before phase 7 in the roadmap, since renumbering would break the docs' many references.

- `screen-space-gi` (Nov 6 2026): bounce light without ray tracing, the visibility bitmask (Therrien, Levesque and Gilet 2023) grown out of GTAO.
  - **Explained first; the user chose** to replace GTAO's loop over a separate pass, since one walk gives both results, and cost on weaker GPUs is this phase's point.
  - **The paper checked** on arXiv's HTML version (the PDF was too big to fetch), through its Algorithm 1 and Table 1. Its timings against GTAO, RTX 2080 at 1080p, sampling alone: 0.75 and 0.77 ms at 12 samples, 1.12 and 1.13 ms at 16, plus 0.3 ms to denoise.
  - **Three changes from the paper:** sectors of equal light, not equal angle (`sectorShare`), so the cosine is in the count and plain occlusion keeps Jimenez's weighting; no emitter cosine, since sectors measure solid angle and the picture is outgoing light; last frame's finished picture, not this frame's direct light, since this pass runs before the main pass, which also gives many bounces free. Its printed line 18 has what looks like a typo in the upper bound, and line 15 subtracts the view direction; the back here is further from the camera.
  - **Added: a surface running on fills its gap,** since a slab per sample leaves a wall seen at a far step full of holes, which plain occlusion would have lost over GTAO's solid horizon.
  - **Found in the running game:** the sun's glint off the steel ball gathered as ordinary light left bright specks on the panel beside it, gone with bounce light off, so each sample is capped (`indirectBrightest`, 8). Mirrors in the rooms showed bright outlines where the probe's search gave up and the flat ambient stood in, so it now falls back to the probe's picture along the ray (`probeGuessedLight`), in the pass only, without ray tracing. Jagged steps at the reflected doorway remain: a head-on mirror is the single probe's worst case, and 1024 texels did not cure it.
  - **Two radii:** `radius` stays 0.5 m for occlusion beside the probes, whose visibility handles what is bigger, and gathering uses `indirectRadius`, 2 m.
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: shares against quadrature, masks, the white furnace adding up to 1, colour taken from the picture, open floor gathering nothing, thin boards letting light behind them, the pass record). The release tests pass, the debug game builds without warnings and the shaders compile. **Not run on a GPU yet:** the user checks it by hand, and `make gputest` and the golden views run only on request. The golden images will differ, since plain occlusion changed too.
  - **Seen on a GPU (Nov 7):** renders on and off in a hidden release copy of the light lab. The difference is small: about 1% of full brightness on average in the rooms, a warm glow beside the doorway from the sunlit floor patch, corners and edges lifted, and no specks round the steel ball, so the cap works. The user: "looks good".
  - **What it leaves:** the user then asked why rooms are brighter than with DDGI. The light from all around is one flat colour for every surface, and the gathering sees only what is on screen within 2 m, so nothing knows a room is closed. That led to the sky visibility grid (next item).

- `planar-reflections` (Nov 6 2026, on the `screen-space-gi` branch): flat mirrors drawn for real. The user, in the light lab: "inside the room reflections look really buggy even with probes on".
  - **Looked at it in the running game:** the debug view showed the probe's search giving up round the reflected doorway; a probe of 1024 texels gave scratchy streaks and no cure, since one depth per direction holds nothing past a door frame.
  - **Asked first; the user chose** softer probe failures, then planar reflections, one plane by default.
  - **How:** a mirror is the capture's seventh view (`captureDraws[cubeFaceCount]`, the capture's pipelines, which already cull front faces), through `planarViewProjection`, whose near plane is the mirror; read at the mirror's own pixel. No sky draw of its own: the camera's rays mirrored (`mirroredRays`). Chosen each frame from `FlatFace`s found at load, so nothing is marked by hand.
  - **Found by the tests:** a list's iterator kept across a `push_back` in `findFlatFaces` crashed the suite.
  - **Its highlights** use the camera's view, not the mirrored eye's.
  - **First seen on a GPU (Nov 7):** both panels a flat white. A debug view showing the picture full screen, in a debug copy of the game with the validation layers, found two bugs. The depth image's view was made as a colour one (`aspect` left at colour; the layers said so). And every pipeline clamps depth (`vulkan_pipeline.cpp`, kept from SDL_GPU) rather than clip at the near plane, so the oblique near plane cut nothing: the wall behind the mirror, flattened onto the near plane, covered the room. The matrix itself, checked on the CPU, was right.
  - **Asked first; the user chose** a clip plane in the vertex shader (`gl_ClipDistance`, `GpuCaptureView`) over capture pipelines without depth clamping (four more pipelines) and a discard in the fragment shader (all the work behind the mirror done and thrown away). The oblique plane then went: the picture keeps the camera's own depths, which the depth measurement can read.
  - **Then its light:** the user saw the mirror's shadows and light "a bit buggy". Everything in it stood in full sun: it shows what is behind the camera, which the camera's cascades never hold. **Asked first; the user chose** cascades of the mirror's own (`MirrorShadowView`: 2 at 2048, from the glass to the farthest surface its picture measured) over widening the camera's cascades to hold both views (blurrier shadows whenever a mirror is in view, and popping as one comes and goes). The camera's last cascade first faded into a band of sunlight before the mirror's row took over, a thin lit stripe on the mirrored floor, so the rows now hand over (`sunCascadeRow`).
  - **Tools for looking:** `--hidden` (a window that never shows) so the AI can drive its own copy of the game with commands without one popping up on the user's screen; the user asked for it.
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first), the debug, release and GPU test programs build without warnings, and the light lab's back room in a hidden debug copy with the validation layers: no errors, the reflected room in shade with its sunlit patch where it really is, from head-on, close up and at angles. 0.29 ms on an RTX 5080 at 1080p. The user saw renders of it and said it looks good. **Not run:** `make gputest` and the golden views (only on request).
  - **Left:** no ambient occlusion or bounce light in the mirror (both come from the screen), and the reflection probe, which still serves every shiny surface that is not the chosen mirror, keeps the same missing shadows behind the camera without ray tracing.

- `sky-visibility` (Nov 7 2026): rooms go dark without light probes. The user, after screen-space GI: "how come the lighting is to bright inside than it was with ddgi tho, that it doesnt seem to get properly dark inside".
  - **Why:** the light from all around was one flat colour for every surface, the sky's light on an open floor, and the screen-space bounce light, reaching 2 m and seeing only the screen, could not tell a closed room from a field.
  - **Asked first; the user chose** a sky visibility grid baked on the CPU, over turning DDGI back on (it needs ray tracing), a longer screen-space reach (still blind to what is off screen) and waiting for phase 14's distance field.
  - **How:** a grid of points in two cascades round the camera (the probes' windows and scrolling reused), each holding how much sky it sees as two spherical harmonic bands, f(n) = a + 2/3 b . n for a surface, baked by 64 rays a point against the engine's own static level (the static, solid, opaque draws), the planes a move brings in within 1 ms a frame. Read like the probes, with each point's reach along the axes against leaks through walls.
  - **Measured first:** a ray against the light lab's 29,632 triangles took 2 microseconds through the default collision grid, 3.1 with 2 m cells once the large-item list every ray tests was counted, 1.35 with 1 m cells and floor-sized triangles alone on it. The grid shrank from the plan's 64 x 32 x 64 to 48 x 24 x 48 points and from 128 rays to 64. The first fill takes 0.49 s on 16 threads; a plane brought in by a move, about 6 ms of all-core time.
  - **Threads:** the uniform grid's mailbox was its own and changed by const queries, so threads could not walk one grid at once; it became `GridMarks`, one per thread.
  - **First look, too bright still:** blocked ways brought back a constant quarter of the sky (the bounce share), so no room could fall below it, and the eye adaptation brightened the rest back. Now the blockers count as lit by twice the point's open share (all of it under an open sky, none in a closed room).
  - **Then glowing seams** (the user's screenshots): with the room dark, every crease glowed, since a crease gathers its neighbours' real brightness from the screen while a flat wall got only the dim sky term. **Asked first; the user chose** letting the flat wall's blocked share take what the screen shows round it (the "fill": ambient occlusion now also averages last frame's picture round each pixel) over a floor on the guess alone, plus a 10% floor so a room seen through a sliver of sky reads dim, not black.
  - **Then swirls and a disc** (the user's screenshot): the fill's few dozen samples a pixel were noisy round bright patches and the upscalers drew swirls; and the fill had gone into the reflection's stand-in too, so a mirror took on the brightness of a bright patch near it, cut off in a disc. The fill now has a blur of its own (two passes, 17 taps each, weighted by view distance carried in the fill's alpha), takes only the first slice's samples, and stays out of reflections. Its cost: about 0.4 ms at 1080p on the RTX 5080 (AO 0.5-0.6 ms without it, 1.0 with).
  - **A crash found on the way:** in the unoptimised debug build a big camera jump baked for 20 seconds inside a frame holding an acquired swapchain image, and the Vulkan validation layer crashed at the next submit (an error dialog no one could see in a hidden copy). The bake now runs before the frame acquires its image, and the debug build compiles the bake and the level's ray code at `-O2`, as it does the cloth. Big jumps, toggles and screenshots then ran clean with the validation layers.
  - **Also:** `floatToHalf` (core/half_float) for packing the points; the debug view keeps the reflection pass out of its grey.
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: the bands, shares and fill, packing, the cascades, reading with points left out, the bake over a floor and in a room with a door, the budget, the grid following the camera, the same bake on four threads as on one, the static level from the draws, walks with their own marks, the half floats, ambient occlusion's fill); the release tests pass, the debug, release and GPU test programs build without warnings, and hidden copies of the light lab showed rooms dark, the sunlit room lit by its patch, outdoors unchanged and no validation errors. **Not run:** `make gputest` and the golden views (only on request); the golden images will differ.
  - **Then the mirrors** (the user: "in the doorway the reflection changes a lot"): the reflection probe's capture lit the rooms behind the camera in full sun, its known lack of shadows past the cascades, hidden until the rooms went dark; the mirror not drawn as the planar one showed a white room. Past the cascades the sun now takes the grid's sky seen towards it as its shadow. The fill's blur grew to 12 taps 4 pixels apart for the last rings.
  - **Left:** faint warm glows round bright things mirrors show (the fill sees the mirror's picture); only the static level counts.

- `ao-temporal` (Nov 7 2026): the screen-space bounce light averaged over frames before the upscalers see it. DLSS turned the gather's per-frame noise (about 36 samples a pixel) into grain and swirls.
  - **Explained first; the user chose** temporal accumulation (option A) over more samples or a wider blur: a running average per pixel, the new frame weighing one over its age up to 16 frames, read where the point was last frame by the camera's reprojection, history texels whose view depth (clip w) does not match the expected one left out.
  - **Decided on the way:** the jitter is not taken off when reprojecting, so a still camera reads exactly its own texel (taking it off and putting last frame's back on would blur the history a little every frame); the age of a partly seen point is its kept taps' bilinear share, so it trusts the history less; no neighbourhood clamp, too wide on a signal this noisy; objects' own motion is not used, since the velocity pass comes after the main pass, and a moving object's pixels start again where the depth test fails.
  - **Cost:** one compute pass of four taps a pixel (not measured yet), and two pairs of images, about 66 MB at 1080p (the depth and age in RG32F, which every GPU can write).
  - **Then the blob** (the user: "it still looks like a big blob of bloom"): screenshots in the user's game with bounce light on and off showed it was the fill, not the noise: the mean of the picture round each pixel, blind to which way the surface faces, so a sunlit floor patch lit the floor in its own plane and smeared onto the walls. Each fill sample now counts only above the surface (`fillFacingWeight`, a ramp from a cosine of 0.1, since depth rounding tilts coplanar samples a little and the fill divides by its weights). That left blotches and streaks over walls and ceilings (the user: "everything looks quite funky now"): one line of samples a frame decides whether anything above the surface is seen, so neighbours jumped between the sunlit floor's light and none, and dividing each frame by a tiny sum made it worse. So the pass now writes the fill's two parts (the light above the surface and the coverage, each a share of every sample's weight), the temporal pass averages them, a small blur (4 taps 2 apart, was 12 taps 4 apart) smooths them, and the main pass divides the averages (a ratio estimator), never by less than 0.02 of coverage (0.05 cut a wall's foot, which a flat line of samples sees at 0.04, by a fifth).
  - **Then the fill came out.** The averaged fill drew dark rounded halos round the openings and light bands near corners. Screenshots in the user's game, one switch at a time: the accumulation off changed nothing, sky visibility or bounce light off removed them, and a 0.7 m reach shrank the bands to hug the corners. So the bands are the reach: only surfaces near another surface get fill, and a wall's middle, whose lit surroundings are the far side of the room, gets none. A screen search cannot see the light from all around a room; shipped engines always put something room-scale underneath screen-space GI (baking, distance fields, voxels, rays). The fill, its blur, its images and set 1 binding 31 were removed, and `skyLightWithFill` with them: the blocked share takes the guess alone until the sky visibility grid carries bounce light (next: its points lit by their rays' hits). The user also saw the walls lag behind a light switch: the 16-frame memory inside the loop through last frame's picture; an anti-lag check comes next.
  - **Then the rings.** Orange arcs stayed on the floor round the sunlit jamb, the same with the accumulation off, finer with 16 steps: the steps' stagger was the slice noise read at a shifted pixel, which for interleaved gradient noise is a fixed function of it, so each slice angle always came with the same sample distances. The stagger now steps on by the golden ratio's fraction a frame (`aoNoise`); a test shows the old pairing leaves at least two quarters of staggers empty for a quarter of angles.
  - **Then the rays.** The arcs became rays fanning from the jamb's foot, moving. Tests in a hidden copy: unchanged by 64 frames of history or by TAA in place of DLSS, finer with 8 slices, dimmer with the brightest sample at 2, gone with a 2 cm thickness. The sectors' rounding (at a half) was dithered first (`sectorMask`'s offset, `aoNoise`'s z), which is right (the bits now average to the arc exactly) but did not remove them. One slice without history showed what they are: the floor that sees the sunlit end of the wall gets its bounce (real), but the face is thin, so a pixel's slice points at it about one frame in twenty and brings back light eight times its surroundings: a rare bright event 16 frames cannot average, lined up as rays. Left for the user to choose: a stronger edge-aware spatial filter, a lower brightest sample, or more slices.
  - **Then the denoiser** (the user chose it over a lower brightest sample or more slices): an edge-aware a-trous filter in place of the 5 x 5 blur, four passes of one 5 x 5 B3-spline kernel 1, 2, 4 and 8 pixels apart, each tap weighted by its distance off the pixel's surface plane over 2% of the view depth (not by view distance, which would cut a sloping floor into bands over 60 pixels) and its normal's cosine to the 32nd, from a geometry image the occlusion pass writes (normal and view depth). The visibility takes the first pass only. Seen in a hidden copy: the rays gone from the far doorway view, a soft glow left close up. Costs 0.65 ms at 1080p on the RTX 5080 (more than the 0.3-0.5 guessed: each tap rebuilds its point with a matrix).
  - **Then what engines do differently** (the user: "what are we doing different than current engines?"): each sample read one texel of the full picture though the far steps are 80 pixels apart (aliasing: shipped SSGI reads colour mips as wide as the samples' spacing), interleaved gradient noise leaves structures where blue noise leaves grain, and nothing cut lone bright samples. Planned in that order. Step 1: each gathered sample reads the reflections' colour pyramid (now built once before the AO pass, `prepareColourPyramid`) at log2 of the steps' spacing (`aoSampleLevel`); in the test a one-pixel stripe's spread over the noise fell fourfold.
  - **Step 2, blue noise.** The pyramid made the ripples fainter but they still moved: the noise's own patterns showing through the averaging. The pass's three noises now come from a 64 x 64 blue noise tile per channel, made by void-and-cluster in C++ at start-up (`core/blue_noise`; a Python tool at 128 would have needed numpy and hours without it), each channel stepping on by its own irrational each frame. In a hidden copy one slice with no averaging showed fine grain where it had shown a wedge of rays; with everything on, a smooth glow.
  - **Then anti-lag** (the user: "now it looks great, but light is still kinda slow"): a 3-frame fast mean of the gathered light's luminance beside the 16-frame one and the slow average of its square; a stray past 3 deviations of the fast mean's own spread restarts the texel. Working the numbers for a trebling showed that testing after this frame's update lets the jump swell the noise estimate and hides the change for two frames, so it tests the fast mean against the history before the update. The tests' hand-made histories (reprojection, rejection) carry no noise estimate, so they run without it. The lag itself could not be judged in a hidden copy: a screenshot settles a fresh history first.
  - **The floor's blobs** (the user: "slightly uneven lighting that forms large blobs on the floor"): captures with the floor's contrast boosted fourfold, one switch at a time: bounce light and AO off changed nothing, sky visibility off made the floor even. The grid's 64 rays a point measure the sky seen coarsely (9 rays through a window against a neighbour's 6), and the floor blends the errors into blobs the grid's size; 256 rays made it a smooth gradient, now the default. Measured in a hidden copy: frames unchanged once settled (8.5 ms at the 120 fps cap, render 2.85 ms against 2.80 at 64), the bake held to its 1 ms a frame; but a whole refill (the first fill, a teleport leaving over half the grid waiting) is four times longer: a 2.6 s frame after a teleport, against about 0.65 s.
  - **The mirror's cascades** at 4096 for stepped sunlit patches in a mirror: tried and reverted to 2048 at the user's request.
  - **Then the flashlight's ghost and the teleport freeze** (Nov 11 2026). Switching the flashlight off left its bounce on the walls for the history's frames: the user confirmed it was the occlusion's history, and anti-lag at 1.5 deviations looked better than 3 but did not cure it. **Asked first; the user chose both** of the two fixes put to them:
    - **The history clip** (variance clipping, Salvi 2016; `historyClip`, 2, 0 off): the history held within this frame's 3 x 3 neighbourhood mean plus or minus 2 deviations, each channel on its own, after the anti-lag test and before the blend. The early "no neighbourhood clamp" choice above was about a box from a single noisy frame's minimum and maximum; a box from the mean and spread is as wide as the noise, so it leaves steady noise alone (tested over 2000 frames: mean within 0.02, spread under 0.08, as without) and drops a uniformly dark neighbourhood's ghost in one frame. About 9 reads a pixel more.
    - **The two-step refill** (`quickRays`, 64): a refill bakes every waiting point at 64 rays, then refines them to 256 within the 1 ms frame budget, oldest first, so a teleport's frame is about as long as at 64 rays (0.65 s against 2.6) and the floor's blobs show for a second or two. The per-frame policy is a pure function, `planSkyBake`; a capture refines everything first, so golden views are unchanged by it.
  - **Then freezes when flying fast** (the user: "moving really far an fast with the cam freezes the game for a bit every few seconds"): the 1 ms budget kept up only to about 10 m/s, and a backlog past half the grid was baked whole in one frame, a rule from `sky-visibility`. The user asked what a plane at 350 m/s would do, then how open-world games handle it: most bake such data offline and stream it with the world, sparse and coarse first, read ahead along the velocity; runtime systems (Lumen's surface cache and distance field clipmaps, DDGI volumes) keep a fixed budget and never stall, updating coarse levels, what is in view and what lies ahead first. **The user chose** the budget alone for moves (no whole bake but for a fresh grid and captures), at the quick rays when behind, the coarsest cascade first and the points ahead nearest first; and an offline, streamed bake for big worlds on the roadmap (phase 14), with the runtime bake kept for what breaks in phase 15.
  - **Then coming back** (the user: "that looks kinda terrible once im moving fast and coming back to the room, maybe we should just bake all of it"): the room's points had scrolled out and were baked again on arrival. Baking the whole light lab at the grid's spacing would be about 2.4 GB at 0.5 m and most of an hour (19 million points, 300 MB and about 7 minutes at 1 m). The user asked how Cyberpunk-sized worlds do it: baked offline at build time, geometry rather than light so the time of day stays free, sparse and adaptive (fine near surfaces, coarse in open air), streamed with the world's sectors (from general knowledge, not checked against sources). **The user chose** keeping every baked point by its place in the world (`SkyCellStore`, 256 MB, least recently used out first), the runtime half of that, with the offline half's roadmap item expanded. Also tried on the way, at the user's request: ray tracing and the light probes on in `settings.cfg` for a look ("looks good"), then off again.
  - **Then a bright wall in the mirror** (the user, with two screenshots: "this wall sees light when i go on the other side of the doorway"). Found by switching things off one at a time in the user's own running game through commands (each restored): the sky grid's debug view showed nothing wrong, fog off changed nothing, reflections off removed it, the reflection probe off did not, planar mirrors off did. So the "openings" were the light lab's mirrors, and the planar mirror's picture lit the wall facing it as open sky. Its shading took the view direction from the real camera, which stood behind that wall, so the grid's lookup, moved towards the viewer, went through the wall. The capture now gets its eye in the push constants (`GpuCaptureView::eye`): the camera reflected across the mirror.
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: the age and share, the depth test, the reprojected place, the exact mean while growing and the spread once full, a white furnace under a moving camera, a sideways step, a disocclusion, partial taps, when the history carries over, the pass record; the clip's box, its neighbourhood, the ghost gone in a frame with it and not without, steady noise kept; the frame's bake plan, quick bakes refined in order, cleared points leaving the queue, coarsest cascade and points ahead first; the store keeping, finding, replacing and dropping the least recently used point, its points packing to the same words, coming back finding them, quick ones refined, a new level or the store off finding none); the release tests pass, the debug and GPU test programs build without warnings. **Not run:** the game (left to the user), `make gputest` and the golden views (only on request).

- `sky-bounce` (Nov 11 2026): the sky visibility grid's rays' hits lit, stage 1 of the grid as CPU light probes. The user, with a screenshot: "all the corners and edges look brighter than the surroundings".
  - **Found first** in a hidden copy of the light lab: with the screen's bounce light off the room was even, with it on corners and edges glowed. Corners gathered their neighbours' real light from the screen, flat walls only the grid's guess for their blocked share (a quarter of twice the open share), far less indoors.
  - **Asked first; the user chose** the grid as CPU light probes (each ray hit lit), in stages, stage 1 first: the sky's bounce. Over a stopgap turning the screen's gather down (which also weakens real bounce) or raising the guess (every room brighter). The write-up: store geometry, not light (bounce per unit of the flat ambient, so the time of day needs no re-bake), albedo per static triangle, the screen-space pass kept on top for near detail.
  - **How:** each static triangle's diffuse albedo beside the level (`StaticGeometry::getAlbedo`: colour decoded, times its texture's decoded mean, times one less metallic; colour, texture and metallic now in the level's fingerprint); each front hit's light from the grid itself (sky plus stored bounce, so bounces add up over refreshes), the point's guess where the grid round the hit is not known; the values per colour channel as two bands (`SkyBounce`); the grid point grown from 16 to 48 bytes (three texels); `ambientFloor` (0.025) in place of the guess's two settings, `bounceShare` and `blockersLitFloor`, which are gone.
  - **Then the first look** (hidden copy, same room after a 20 m jump): little changed, since points baked as a move brought them in found nothing baked round their hits and kept the guess. Such points are now marked unsure and baked once more through the refine queue; hits past the grid do not count (waiting would not help). Measured after: the ceiling edge 38 against 37 below it (was clearly brighter), the corner by the window still 46 against the wall's 38: the sun's bounce, which the screen sees and the grid will carry in stage 2.
  - **Cost, measured on the laptop** (RTX 4070 Laptop, 16 threads): a whole grid at full rays with bounce about 3-4 s (a screenshot, which bakes it, 4-5 s); frames after a jump did not stall. A run right after a fresh build took minutes, which the user explained: the driver rebuilds its compiled shaders on the first start, and the game does not respond meanwhile (a loading screen asked for next).
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: the light from all around in an open field with and without the ground's bounce, a closed box and its floor, the clamps, packing the bounce, reading it back with what was known and missing, the light on a hit and its guess, the bounce over a floor against the analytic answer, none in a closed dark room or inside a lone block, points baked too early baked again once, two batches the same on one thread and four, each triangle's albedo from colour, texture and metal, the fingerprint); the release tests pass, the debug, release and GPU test programs build without warnings. **Not run:** `make gputest` and the golden views (only on request); the golden images will differ.
