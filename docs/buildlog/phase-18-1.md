# Build log: Phase 18: A jungle valley showcase (Nov 13 2026 onwards) (part 1 of 3)

## Phase 18: A jungle valley showcase (Nov 13 2026 onwards) (part 1 of 3)

- `forest-assets` (Nov 13 2026): the forest's scanned assets. The user asked for something lush, realistic and free (CC0), so a survey of Poly Haven and ambientCG came first, as a shortlist page; the user took the recommended set ("sounds good").
  - **What:** 30 assets, 142 files, 157 MB at 1K. 17 Poly Haven models (ferns, plants, shrubs, nettles, a stump, dead trunks, a root, mossy rocks, boulders), 8 Poly Haven textures (forest floor, muddy leaves, mossy rock, two barks, three sci-fi metals), and 5 ambientCG sets (grass and moss grounds, three leaf and foliage atlases).
  - **No scanned trees:** Poly Haven's are 2 to 17 million triangles each, far too heavy for a forest of them, so the engine will grow its own: generated trunks and branches in scanned bark, with ambientCG's leaf atlases on cut-out cards.
  - **Kept out of git:** `tools/fetch_forest_assets.py` (standard library only) downloads them into git-ignored `assets/forest`, Poly Haven's through its API and ambientCG's as zips cached in `build/downloads/forest`. Each file is checked against the SHA-256 recorded in `tools/forest_assets.json` (`--record` writes them), written under a temporary name first, and fetched only when missing.
  - **1K only:** the engine has no texture compression yet, so larger textures would cost video memory for little gain at a walking distance.
  - **Checked:** a second run found all 30 present and matching their checksums.
- **Blended grounds** (Nov 13 2026, `forest-assets`): one ground mesh painted with up to four ordinary materials by a weights texture, the user's ask ("blending is important").
  - **Height blending** (Mishkinis 2013) over a plain cross-fade: each painted layer's weight times how far its height map comes within the blend depth of the highest painted one's, normalised. Chosen so a layer not painted at a place never shows there, whatever its height, and equal heights keep the painted weights exactly.
  - **The layers are materials in the table,** named by index plus one, so the main pass reads them like any other material, and two grounds sharing a layer share its record.
  - **A trap found while wiring the forest:** a layer without a normal map read the white stand-in texture, decoded as a 45-degree tilt, and one without a height map stood highest. The shader now keeps such a layer flat and halfway up.
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first), the GPU test case compiled (not run: GPU tests on request).
- **The forest game** (Nov 13 2026, `forest-assets`): `--game forest`, the land, its ground, the sun and the mist.
  - **The land** is pure functions of the place, so drawing, walking and paint agree, and tests can ask any point. Value noise from the engine's hash, no library.
  - **The first land was too gentle:** nothing steeper than 33 degrees outside the path's cuttings, so no rock would show. Taller knolls (7 m over 28 m) gave about 1.4% rock.
  - **The path then got too steep,** since it followed the land. Taking the broad hills alone as its level ramped it into the clearing by however much the knolls lift the clearing's centre. Its level is now the land averaged over 12 m either way along it (at most about 16 degrees), levelled with the clearing.
  - **Stand-ins:** plain coloured layers where `assets/forest` is missing, so a fresh clone runs and the tests need no downloads.
  - **`advanceSolarHours`** moved from the light lab to `scene/sun`, as both games' clocks use it.
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: the noise, the clearing, hollows and path, the mesh, the paint by place, the weights texture, layer files by role, the game headless, settings each frame); the debug game builds without warnings. Not run: the game itself (left to the user); golden references not saved.

  - **Can the engine do it?** The light, materials, fog and image quality are there.   - **The plan:** a rough valley first (grey pillars, islands and slabs at real size, measured on the laptop with the typical GPU setup), then the scale items in the order it shows (aerial perspective, far terrain, level of detail, impostors, occlusion culling, texture compression, distant shadows), then more assets (a survey first), the pillars and islands, trees, ground cover, the structures, wind. Level of detail and texture compression moved from phase 12, impostors and Hi-Z occlusion culling from phase 14.

- `rough-valley` (Nov 13-14 2026): the rough valley, the scale test, built; its measurement is the user's, on the laptop with the typical GPU setup.
  - **Explained first, then the user's choices:** the floating islands still at first and moving later (a 300 m caster moving every frame redraws its shadow every frame); the machines keep the islands up, the story hook.
  - **The land:** a valley 150 m deep and 700 m across at the rim, a ramp from the rim to the floor, mountains beyond, the first forest's hills and knolls over all of it. The path at first climbed at up to 0.65 (33 degrees): the knolls on the ramp. Averaging the path's level over 30 m instead of 12, and a ramp 70% straight instead of eased, brought it to 0.43 (23 degrees), under the test's 0.5 and well under the walker's 45 degrees.
  - **Rings, not one mesh:** the land to 20 km as one 2 m grid would be 400 million squares. Four nested squares, each 4 times wider than the last (the last 2), with squares 8, 4 and 4 times coarser, give 620,800 squares, 1.24 million triangles; a ring's border follows the next ring's edge exactly, so nothing cracks. Only the finest ring is static, as kilometres of land in the static level's 1 m cells would slow the sky visibility grid's rays.
  - **Shapes from one grid builder:** `wrappedSurfaceMesh` makes pillars, islands and the torus. A trap: cos(pi/2) in float is -4e-8, so the pillar's top row sat a hair past the axis, flipping a ring of tiny triangles inwards; clamped to zero. The first "faces outwards" test was wrong at an island's tip (the tip row's middle is above the underside), so the test now checks sideways-facing triangles against their row and the whole surface's signed volume.
  - **The engine hook:** `Game::addCommands`, so a game adds its own commands (the user's rule: every feature runs from the command line); `forest-view rim|ring|wall`.
  - **Measured (the laptop, release):** building the valley took 5.1 s on one thread; its ground rows on the job system brought it to 1.7 s on 16 threads. The golden views test builds it on one thread four times, so `make test-release` went to about 151 s here.
  - **Checked:** the tests (release); the debug game builds without warnings. Not run: the game (the user's look and the measurements); golden references not saved.
  - **The user's look** (Nov 14 2026): "that works". Merged; the haze's colour, the far cascades and the frame time still to be read off for the next item's order.
- **What the rough valley showed** (Nov 14 2026, the user's screenshots on the laptop): the haze turns blue, so aerial perspective waits; 7.4 ms of GPU from the rim with the typical setup (12.95 at Ultra with DLAA), so level of detail, impostors and occlusion culling wait for the trees; the sun's shadows stop at 200 m (`ShadowSettings::directionalDistance` and its caster reach), so every far pillar, island and tower is lit on all sides, a large part of why the distance looked flat; and "looks a bit square in some places". The roadmap was re-ordered: the ground's paint, distant shadows, then the content.
- `ground-paint` (Nov 14 2026): the squares were the ground's paint. One weights texture of 40 m texels for every far ring, under a blend sharpened by the layers' height maps, turned rock patches into blocks with a corner at every texel.
  - **A texture per ring** over its own square: 2048 over the walked-on ring (0.625 m texels, was 1.25), 1024 over each coarser one (5, 20 and 40 m). About 28 MB with mips.
  - **Softer where coarse:** each ring's blend depth is the setting's times the square root of how many times coarser its texels are than the finest, at most 1, so far patches fade into each other.
  - **Measured (the laptop, release):** the valley builds in 2.4 s on 16 threads (1.7 before).
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first); the debug game builds without warnings. Not run: the game (the user's look).
  - **Still square** (the user's look, Nov 14 2026). Baking the near paint to an image on the CPU showed its edges smooth at the texel size, but its shapes square: rock patches and openings running along x and z. Value noise interpolates along its lattice's axes, so its slopes do too: 47% of one octave's slopes lay within 10 degrees of an axis, 32% of the summed octaves', against 22% for random directions. Each octave is now turned and shifted its own way (the first by atan(3/4), each next by the golden angle more): 21.6%, and the baked paint's shapes wander. A test holds the share under 25%. The path stays at most 0.43.
  - **Still square, again** (the user's look after the noise): "looks equally square". The rock's borders were staircases a texel a step. A texture read straight blends the four nearest texels along straight lines, whose slope jumps at every texel centre; the height blend, sharp on purpose, turns the weights into an edge right there, so the edge follows those kinks along the texture's grid however fine the texels. Baking the paint to an image had hidden it, as the image was looked at enlarged, not through the blend. The weights are now read as a cubic B-spline (`groundPaintTaps`, four straight reads; Sigg and Hadwiger 2005), whose slope is continuous, so the borders are smooth curves. Tests: the two reads give the B-spline's value, and its slope does not jump across a texel where the straight read's does (0.7); a GPU test case against the mirror (compiled, not run). The finer textures and the turned noise stay: the first helps the far rings, the second took out the land's own square grain.
  - **The user's look** (Nov 14 2026): "its better, under some grass it will be fine". Merged.

- `rock-strata` (Nov 14 2026): the pillars' and islands' rock. Explained first; the user asked for the trade-offs and how other games do it, then chose triplanar mapping in the object's frame and one layer cake for the valley ("yes please").
  - **Why not texture coordinates:** 16 meshes are drawn 250 times, stretched to 12-45 m wide and 200-350 m tall, so one texture would cover 190 m round and 300 m up, a different size on every pillar. Per-pillar meshes with coordinates in metres were weighed (one read a texture, parallax works) and dropped: the radius changes along a pillar shear them, ledge tops and domes take the sides' mapping smeared, the islands' cones pinch, and nothing is reusable for the structures. Generated geometry is where other engines use triplanar mapping too (voxel and procedural worlds); hand-made rocks use artists' coordinates plus a layer projected from above.
  - **In the object's frame, not the world's:** the islands are to move later, and a world projection would slide over them. The frame is the model matrix's rotation, the positions scaled to metres, passed from the vertex shader.
  - **The strata are by world height,** so the same bed stands at the same height on every pillar, as real eroded sandstone's does; a moving island's beds would slide up and down (to fix with a per-instance anchor when they move).
  - **Built on the ground's blending:** projection, strata and the top layer are options of the layered material, so the height blend, packing and layer reads are shared. The non-projected path now reads its layers with `textureGrad` too (the same mips as the bias gave).
  - **Cost:** a projection's share under 0.1 is dropped, so most pixels read one or two of the three. Biplanar reads (always two) are the fallback if the frame cost shows.
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: the frame, the shares, places and normals against ordinary normal mapping, the strata and top layer, the table, the cake, its strip, the game's rock, its settings each frame); the GPU test cases written (not run: GPU tests on request); the debug and release games build without warnings. Not run: the game (the user's look).
  - **The user's look** (Nov 14 2026): the beds lined up and the rock kept its size, but the pillars read as a striped cake: three very different rocks (one, ambientCG's procedural `Rock061`, flat and bluish) in 15-20 thin beds up a pillar, with ruled edges.   - **Carved shapes:** every pillar and island its own mesh in metres, a row and a column every 2.5 m, ledges where the hard beds stand (2 m out, the soft rock under them cut back 1.5 m) at the very heights the shader paints them, narrow vertical joints 2.5 m deep every 9 m or so, a broken flat top. Hex tiling with offsets only (turning would tilt the sandstone's layers), most places one or two taps; unpainted layers now skipped, which most rock pixels gain more from than hex tiling costs.
  - **The ground too** ("the ground still look repetitive"): hex tiling switched on for every ring of ground (`ForestGroundSettings::hexTiling`); the black streaks the user asked about are both the water streaks (narrow, vertical) and the carved rock's own shadows (broad, diagonal).

  - **Drawn-only meshes:** about 3 million triangles of rock would keep over 500 MB as `Mesh`es (168 bytes a triangle, kept all run); `AssetManager::addDrawnMesh` keeps them in the render format instead, about 120 MB. The trees will need the same.

No: past the fog's grid (100 m) the height fog's closed form was lit by the sun everywhere, and with its forward scattering (g 0.6) the air between the eye and the sun is the brightest there is, so the mist in the pillar's shadow glowed. Explained three fixes (marching the far shadow map along each pixel's ray; stretching the grid to a kilometre or two, coarser near the camera and still needing the far map; screen-space light shafts, which cannot darken the air in front of the pillar); the user chose the march.

  - **In the picture pass, at full resolution,** rather than a smaller image of its own as first described: no new images or upsampling at silhouettes, the samples turned per pixel and frame and averaged by the anti-aliasing after it. `farShadowSteps` (16, F5 Fog, 0 off) sets the cost.

- `drifting-rocks` (Nov 14 2026): the user chose bobbing rocks that keep their far shadows ("so we get nice rays of light through the mist") and asked for some round the pillars too.

  - **One bed's tones:** the rock's beds are painted by world height, which would slide over a bobbing rock; the drifting rocks' strip is a single soft bed, so nothing slides, while the moss and streaks, projected in the rock's own frame, ride with it.
  - **No joints:** the islands' shape cuts joints 2.5 m deep, which would split a 3 m rock; the carving's joint depth is now a field (`RockCarving::jointMetres`), 0 for these.
  - **Clear of everything:** rocks are thrown round their own shape and dropped if they would touch any other pillar or island.

A rock moves at most about a metre in that time, under half a 2.7 m texel. Anything arriving, leaving or changing redraws it at once; the cascades stay exact. The far map's refresh went to half a second: at up to about 6 m/s a rock moves about a texel in that time.
