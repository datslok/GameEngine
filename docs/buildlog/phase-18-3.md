# Build log: Phase 18: A jungle valley showcase (Nov 13 2026 onwards) (part 3 of 3)

## Phase 18: A jungle valley showcase (Nov 13 2026 onwards) (part 3 of 3)

- `plant-fades` (Nov 18 2026): the scanned understorey plants' levels and cards cross-faded by a dither over a band of distance instead of swapped (research `docs/research/LOD.md`, Hiding the switches, item 2)
  - **Distance-based, not time-based (the user's choice):** a pure function of the view and the draw, so it slots into the GPU's candidates (both slots of a fading draw are already candidates) and the CPU's reference; Unreal's 0.5 s timed fade needs per-draw state kept between frames, reset whenever the still draws rebuild, which `cull-compare` could not follow. Its known cost: a plant standing in the band stays dithered, left to TAA and DLSS to blend.
  - **The band in the budget, not in metres:** a coarser level fits pixelError / (1 - band) a band's width before its switch, so one setting (0.2) scales with each level's error and the screen; the share is measured by the coarser's error, so a plant and its card fade by one number and split every pixel.
  - **Complementary pixels:** the coarser keeps noise below the field's step middle, the finer from it; 63 steps, never all or none, in 7 bits of the draw list entry, leaving 24 bits (16.7 M) of instance, checked.
  - **The prepass decides:** the main pass already shades only what the depth prepass kept (EQUAL), so only `cut_out_prepass.frag` (after its texture reads, keeping their derivatives) and the velocity pass discard. Hence cut-out materials only (the three scans and the cards are).
  - **Records read only for fading entries:** the flags pass marks a fading candidate with one more bit (`CAMERA_CULL_FLAG_BITS` 3 to 4) and the write pass works its field out again from the record, rather than spending 7 bits of the candidate's place.
  - **The camera only:** captures, the mirror and shadow tiles keep swapping (a turning dither in a shadow map would shimmer); the palms keep their card thinning.
  - **Tested:** the CPU's `levelFade` by hand-worked distances and a plant into its card; the packed choice equal to the CPU's over random scenes and views; the GPU's lists equal to `batchByMesh`'s entry for entry with fade fields; every pixel kept by exactly one level. GPU tests written (`level_fade_gpu_test`, `cull_records_gpu_test` cases), not run.
- `fog-layers` (Nov 18 2026): up to four fog layers adding, exponential or pooled, and the jungle's floor mist pooled under the canopy (research `docs/research/FOGLAYERS.md`)
  - **Layers add (Frostbite, Unreal's second fog layer):** extinctions sum; scattered light weighs each layer's albedo and phase by its share of the extinction at the point (froxels, far samples) or of the stretch's depth (the unshadowed far fog: exact only for alike layers, which mist and haze are).
  - **Pooled shape, not a second exponential (the user's choice, option B over Unreal's two exponentials):** even up to a top, falling by e per edge above, so the mist tops out under the canopy (12 m + 4 m edge under the canopy trees' 18 m forks, 0.03 per metre). Its closed form takes the part above the top from its lower end, so a ray from 10 km up stays finite.
  - **Newton for the far samples:** a sum of layers has no closed inverse; 8 bracketed steps from the guess exact for even fog. A first version halved away from a right guess when rounding put the step on the bracket's edge (sample at 117 m for 102 m); steps onto the edge are now taken. One exponential layer keeps the closed form.
  - **Sun dimmed through the fog (the user asked for it):** exp(−depth) towards each directional light per froxel and per far sample, one closed form per layer; a setting (`sunThroughFog`, on). It dims the haze's light too: about 16% with the sun 60° up, most of it at sunset. Then surfaces too (the user asked), with the similarity relation (the user chose it over the full extinction): the depth times 1 − albedo × g, since droplets deflect light only a little and a deflected photon still lights the ground (`forwardShare`, 1; 0 the full extinction). Under 14 m of mist at noon: 17% dimmer instead of 38%; the haze 7% instead of 16%. Shared through `scene_fog_data.glsl`, so surfaces and ray hits read the record without the fog's grid.
  - **Far shadow samples placed once per pixel** instead of once per light, now that each carries the layers' shares and the sun's dimming.
  - **Tested:** the pooled closed form against fine sums for rays crossing the top both ways, level, nearly level and from far above; layers adding, shares, the sun's transmittance; Newton's samples reaching their depths on five rays; packing; the forest's two layers and their switches. GPU tests written (`volumetric_fog_gpu_test` medium of both shapes, packed layers), not run.
- `loading-screen` (Nov 19 2026): the world built behind the loading screen instead of a black window for about 10 s at start
  - **Cause:** `game.onInit` ran on the main thread before the loading screen's first frame, which only showed while pipelines finished (usually done by then).
  - **Background thread, not progress calls from the game:** `runWhileWaiting` runs onInit on its own thread while the main thread draws and answers the window (as Unreal's and Unity's loading screens do), needing no change in any game; games are headless and the job system takes work from any thread. A game reporting stages could come later for a real count.
  - **Sliding block:** the world's load cannot count, so a quarter-bar block slides end to end in 45 frames (about 1.5 s); the count's line stays empty so the screen does not jump when the shaders' count begins.
  - **Closing during the load** cannot stop onInit: drawing stops and the game quits once it ends. Errors from onInit are rethrown on the main thread as before.
  - **Measured (laptop, hidden copy, release), the user then saw 2-3 s black and a frozen bar:** start-up 25 s: window and renderer 3.3 s (Streamline, device and swapchain 2.2, DLSS 0.6), world 8.3, meshes 2.9, first frame 10.3 (its scene build 6.6, its sky bake 3.5). Kept as log lines (`core/startup_clock`: "Start-up", "Renderer start", the first frame's renderer stages).
  - **Then:** the window made hidden and shown with its first loading frame; the meshes uploaded in 33 ms slices between loading frames, counted; the first frame's scene and sky bake behind the screen on their own thread (the scene reads only the world and assets, the bake only its CPU grid). First frame drawn after them: 0.14 s. The 10 s of first-frame CPU itself not made shorter: a later job.
  - **Tested:** the helper (quick work, waiting while working, the work's and the waiting side's errors, the work always finished first) and the budgeted steps; the start-up clock's laps and line; the layout's titles, the block's path and its place inside the bar.
- `structure-seams` (Nov 19 2026): the structures' panel lines and sawtooth seams, stage 2a of their weathering (plan `docs/research/STRUCTURES.md`, Planned: weathering)
  - **Facet projection, not triplanar:** the hexagon sections turn facets every which way, so three axis projections cross-fade a straight seam on a facet turned 30 degrees and draw it twice. One read in the facet's own plane: across level, up the height on steep facets, so a seam at one height meets itself round every edge like a floor line. One read a layer instead of up to three.
  - **A third projection mode, not a second flag:** `Material::groundProjected` became `groundProjection` (texture coordinates, axes, facets); facets set `GROUND_PROJECTED` too, so streaks and tone variation work on them with one read each.
  - **Painted by strata, one texel:** the facets' texture coordinates are their two random numbers, so a weights texture over them would read noise.
  - **No per-facet offset of the tile:** each triangle has its own numbers, so offsetting by them would break seams along every quad's diagonal; the 16 m tile's irregular panels and random row starts carry the variety.
  - **Generated, not scanned (the user's choice):** rows 2-4 m, panels 2-6 m, sawtooth edges at 45 degrees with teeth dividing the tile so they meet across its edges, hatches; grooves 3 cm, 2 cm bevels, 2048 texels over 16 m (0.8 cm). The height image is kept for 2b's moss blend. Normals by the existing `normalMapFromHeight`.
  - **Tested:** facet axes orthonormal and right-handed, places level round steep facets and unstretched on flat ones, linear; normals and streaks; packing; the layout's sizes and sawtooth; distances, wrapping and teeth; the images' grooves, bevel normals and seamless edge; the forest's coating and its live settings. GPU test written (`ground_layers_gpu_test` facet case), not run.

- `slit-windows` (Nov 20 2026): the towers lit at night, stage 4 of the structures: slit windows, light strips on the major fins, floodlights washing the Bastion's walls (research `docs/research/STRUCTURES.md`, "Lights at night")
  - **Windows as a mask, not geometry:** a procedural mask in the facet's plane (the formwork's coordinates), its cost growing with the tower's pixels (about 30 ALU), not with the windows; mirrored (`window_mask.glsl`, `render/gpu/window_mask`), CPU and GPU tested.
  - **Box-filtered, then their average:** a 0.6 m slit is under a pixel past about 300 m; the mask integrates the slits over the pixel's footprint (the running cover of periodic pulses) and, past a cell a pixel, fades each window's own state to its expected value, so a far band glows at its true average instead of flickering under TAA. Tested: the near windows over a wall average to the far value.
  - **Bands as a strip over height, like the strata:** each tower's rows generated from its shape into an exact 8-texels-a-metre strip; mips average the cover. The shares are carried into the blank wall between rows, or the mips would dilute them twice (cover x share both averaged with zeros) and far bands would come out too dark.
  - **Seeds from the facet numbers, rounded:** interpolating three equal corners can move a float by an ulp, which would scramble a hash pixel by pixel; rounding to 1024 steps holds it.
  - **Military, not flats:** pairs of rows every 40 m and a visor band on the head, openings under 2 % of the wall; the command pair over each setback lit more.
  - **Strips as geometry:** 1.5 m bars on the major fins, so they stay about a pixel at 1.5 km; emissive, live brightness.
  - **Floods as real spot lights (the user's choice over a painted glow):** 18 unshadowed spots on the ground before the glacis and on the ledges, so the fins catch the light and the slots stay dark; cost grows with lights x the pixels they reach.
  - **`GpuMaterial` 288 to 320 bytes:** the windows' two colours and pitch; the strip's slot in the unused `groundSlots.w`.
- `fog-fast-lights` (Nov 20 2026): the fog stops trailing behind the flashlight (research `docs/research/FOGHISTORY.md`)
  - **Cause:** each froxel keeps 90 % of last frame's light, so a moving light settles over about ten frames, a few tenths of a second at the forest's frame rate.
  - **What shipped engines do:** the same blend (0.86-0.95) and accept the trail; Unreal and Godot document turning such lights out of the fog; Unity's shader has a to-do to keep dynamic lights out of the history; a per-froxel clamp only in a Frostbite thesis.
  - **Chosen (option C, the user's choice):** lights flagged fast lit fresh each frame into a grid of their own, added by the integration; the history keeps only the rest. A mask of fast slots in the fog record (`markFastFogLights`; `GpuFog` 400 to 560 bytes); a setting to compare (`freshFastLights`).
  - **At the froxel's middle, unjittered:** without the history's averaging a jittered sample would flicker; the middle is the same every frame, so the beam's edge is only as fine as the froxels.
  - **Cost:** a second RGBA16F grid (160 x 90 x 64, 7.4 MB), one more texel read a froxel in the integration; the fast lights' shading the same as before.
  - **Left for later:** other moving lights (searchlights, sweeping floods) still trail; a 6-neighbour clamp of the history if they do (ROADMAP).

- `obstruction-lights` (Nov 19 2026): the towers' red aircraft-warning lights, stage 5 of the structures (research `docs/research/STRUCTURES.md`, "Planned: obstruction lights")
  - **The rules:** FAA L-864 / ICAO type B, 2000 cd red, 30 flashes a minute about 0.5 s on, all on a structure in unison, levels at most about 105 m apart; the Bastion gets 6 levels of 4.
  - **Drawn as the stars are, not as sprites of fixed size:** E = I / d^2 shared by weights summing to 1, so a beacon keeps its light from metres to kilometres (fixed-size sprites, Flight Simulator's failure, brighten with distance); the star's tent under a pixel, the lens's disc past two, mixed between. The disc's sum is within 2.5 % of 1 (its edge a pixel-wide ramp).
  - **The flash on the CPU, not in the shader:** the game's clock reaches no shader, and the same level drives the splat and the red spill light on the concrete, so the two never drift apart. Timed by the simulation clock between ticks (`simulationSeconds` is a tick's end), frame-only.
  - **After the fog is applied, before the glass:** fogged at its own point (`fogAlongView` in the fragment shader; the picture behind was fogged to its own, maybe the sky's, depth); glass then refracts it. No reactive mask yet: the stars, also sub-pixel, need none.
  - **Far towers out of step** (the user's choice): each its own 20-40 a minute and phase, a scale cue across kilometres; the Bastion's spill 30 within 20 m pulses red on its corners (unshadowed, fast point lights).
  - **Cost:** six vertices a beacon on screen (252 in the valley: 24 on the Bastion, 228 on the far towers) and a fog lookup a covered pixel; the spill grows with the pixels its 24 lights reach.
  - **Tested:** the flash's timing, phases, steady and always-on cases; spill lights following the level; the gathered light; the weights' sums, the light at the eye at 20 m to 8 km, a near lens's luminance; the records; the levels' spacing and corners on the Bastion and a far tower; the forest's beacons, rates, flashes and live intensity. - `valley-maps` (Nov 19 2026): the valley's planting maps, stage 1 of the rest of the valley (research and the user's choices `docs/research/VALLEY.md`); the patch replanted from them
  - **Maps, not darts** (Horizon Zero Dawn): ecotopes (floor, wall, rim, bare), a clearance distance field and a canopy cap a species' reach, 2 m texels over 0.9 x 5.8 km: about 8.4 MB, about 0.1 s at start-up (release, laptop RTX 4070 machine, on the jobs). A layer's density is a function of them plus noise gaps.
  - **Ordered dither, not darts or white noise:** Roberts' R2 thresholds over each layer's lattice in 32-bit whole numbers (the same on a shader later); layers sharing a lattice take bands of thresholds (emergents under canopy), so never one cell. Every test is local: any cell is planted alone, as stage 2 needs.
  - **Clearance as a distance field:** one map for every reach (pillars, the Ring's feet, the towers), read less the texel's reach so it stays a lower bound.
  - **Caps exact per texel, not dilated:** a first version rasterised the lines and grew them by a square of whole texels, which widened a 6 m crown's corridor to about 10 m and left the mid layer under 900; the exact lowest point of each line within reach of the texel's middle (where it enters and leaves the disc) is cheaper and only half a texel's diagonal conservative.
  - **Densities asked, not stood:** without darts' retries, crowding (`spacingShare`), trunks, the path, gaps and corridors drop cells, so the per-hectare figures were raised to keep the patch's count: canopy 30 (79 stand), emergents 2.8 (9), mid 480 (1,212); understorey 8,640 (about 10,000 before: gaps, obstacles and the corridors' 2 m at eye height), palms 721.
  - **Gaps** (noise over 70 m, about 13 % of the patch): no canopy or emergents, 0.4 of the mid layer, palms twice and the understorey 1.5 times as thick (gap dynamics: palms fill a gap first).
  - **Not yet on the GPU:** the maths is plain functions of maps and numbers, ready for stage 2's compute shader and its C++ mirror; its `.glsl` library comes with the stage that runs it.
  - **Tested:** the ecotopes from the land and read back, the maps the same on one core as on many, the clearance and caps as lower bounds within a texel or two, nothing that fits meeting a sight line or an obstacle, the dither's evenness and spread, a layer's share and cells (count, jitter, region, path, bands), the obstacles; the trees', palms' and understorey's placements on the maps.
- `gpu-placement` 2a (Nov 19 2026): the placement maths for both sides, stage 2's first step (research and the user's choices `docs/research/VALLEY.md`, Stage 2's design)
  - **The GPU decides, every layer (the user's choice):** weighed against the CPU placing per cell and the GPU only building; the CPU's cost grows with ground entering range times density (phase 15's flight, teleports, the far tiers), the GPU's stays far under a millisecond.

  - **Generic, in `scene/`:** games never include the renderer and the renderer never a game, so the maths, the maps' layout and the layers' planting live in `scene/planting*`; the forest builds the maps and turns its species into records.
  - **Maps for what needed a sine or a root:** the axis a row (read between rows), the gaps (their noise baked, 1.3 MB) and the path's distance (2.6 MB); the maps now about 12 MB.

The density blends by the map; the clearance tests the path's nine points' pieces exactly.

- `gpu-placement` 2b1 (Nov 19 2026): the cells round the camera planted on the GPU and compared with the CPU, nothing drawn yet (the user's split of 2b)
  - **Cells planted alone, not one shared field:** each 64 m cell plants every layer over its own grown boxes (`plantingLayerBoxes`, now shared by `plantLayers` and the jobs), so a cell needs no neighbour; the borders cost repeated work at the cells' edges, the price of cells arriving in any order.
  - **Fixed slots, made a few a frame:** the ring drops cells a cell past 300 m and makes the nearest missing ones, 4 a frame, into the lowest free slot; 169 slots, so no allocation while moving; a teleport refills over a few dozen frames.
  - **Three passes a layer, no atomics in the plants:** candidates, spacing, then a prefix sum writing each cell's kept plants in rows from the north, so the GPU's order is `plantLayers`' and the comparison is plant by plant; atomics only count problems.
  - **Candidates kept per cell made, not per slot:** later layers read earlier layers' kept marks through the same cell's job, and the scratch is rewritten whole by the next frame's candidates, so it is never cleared.
  - **The ground exact on both sides:** `terrain_heights.glsl` split out of `terrain_data.glsl` with its buffer's binding chosen by the includer, multiplying by the CPU's reciprocal spacing and `precise`; the drawn ground-following meshes read the same.
  - **One upload helper:** `gpuBufferHolding` replaces the renderer's two hand-written staging copies.
  - **Cost:** grows with the cells made a frame; memory 16.8 MB of slots for the forest's layers (the understorey's 1.5 m lattice is 71 % of a slot), the field's maps about 12 MB.
  - **Tested:** the ring (nearest first, follows a walk, the same twice, teleports, resets), the slots' room for any cell, cells planting the whole, the maps' packing round trip, layers, jobs, comparison and command. `placement-compare` in the forest (hidden copies, release and debug): every plant of every layer equal, bit for bit, at the start (88 cells, 48,710 plants) and after 800 m and 1.5 km moves; no problems counted; PLANTING 0.08 ms on a frame making 4 cells (release, laptop RTX 4070); debug validation clean but the 9 known start-up warnings.
- `gpu-placement` 2b2 (Nov 19 2026): the placed trees drawn over the patch in the camera's prepass and main pass, behind the forest's `placedTrees` (the user's split of 2b; the shadow choice left to me)
  - **Shadows from the entity stand-ins while placed trees draw (my pick, A):** the toggle then changes only the trees, so it compares like with like; placed parts cast nothing until 2c.
  - **A part's record in the plant's frame, moved per plant:** the camera's own `cameraCandidateFlags` runs on each placed part's record (`placedCullRecord`), so levels, impostors, fades and thinning choose as for entities; tested against the entity draws through the CPU's `batchByMesh`.
  - **The passes' pipelines, not their own:** the vertex shader branches on the draw's first instance (`PLACED_INSTANCE_BASE`), the same for a whole draw, instead of a second set of prepass and main pipelines compiled at start-up; the roadmap line said own pipelines and was rewritten.

  - **Counts, then places, no atomics in the entries:** a block counts each group, a scan per group places the blocks, one workgroup the groups; the writing pass ranks a plant among its block's earlier ones, so the GPU's order is `cullPlacedOnCpu`'s and `placement-compare` compares entry by entry.

  - **The entity's matrix step for step:** `placedPlantModel` repeats `Transform::getMatrix` with the quaternion's zeros left out, equal on the CPU; the GPU's sine and cosine round differently.
  - **The patch's edge from the maps' axis:** the forest's own `PlantingRegion` (its curve) gave way to `PlantedRegion` read from the maps, so the GPU, which has only the maps, draws exactly the entity trees' patch.
  - **One look a variant:** the tree entities are made from the same `PlantedKindLook`s the placed trees draw, so their parts cannot drift apart.
