# Build log: Phase 7: Cloth simulation (Oct 24 2026 onwards) (part 3 of 3)

## Phase 7: Cloth simulation (Oct 24 2026 onwards) (part 3 of 3)
- `dress-yardstick` (Oct 26 2026): the dress-sized test, phase 7's fourth step. Does a garment need more than XPBD and tethers? Yes.
  - **Explained first; the user chose** four resolutions (2, 1.4, 1 and 0.7 cm, about 3,000 to 25,000 points), since the error shows in how it grows, and a pass mark set before measuring: 1% strain at the 99th percentile at 1 cm, about where stretch starts to show in cotton. Vertex block descent and its levels were dropped from the test at the user's request ("unacceptable performance": 8-17 times XPBD on the CPU), leaving the dress held by contact alone against it pinned at the shoulders and tethered, with XPBD on the CPU and the GPU.
  - **New measures for a garment,** which has no columns or corners to measure along (`measureGarment`): the mean stretch (squashed edges counting as none), the 99th percentile strain (where it carries its weight, without the odd pinched edge), the worst edge, the widest seam, the hem's height and the nearest any point comes to the body.
  - **One dress for the lab and the test:** `dressOnMannequin` makes it, `prepareDressTick` readies each tick (weightless while sewing, the strap pins carried), `mannequinField` builds the body's field; the lab's own copies of these went. `settleYardstick` takes the fields and a preparation each tick, and the runner's CPU and GPU settlings are shared by the sheets and the dress (the GPU's tick commands now taken per tick, after the preparation).
  - **Measured: it fails at every rung, held either way** (docs/CLOTHGPU.md, Part 5). Held by contact, the mean stretch went 0.32, 0.54, 0.96 and 2.0% from 2 cm to 0.7 cm, the 99th percentile 4.4 to 19%, the hem 0.598 to 0.531 m. Pinned and tethered it was no better: the mean stretch 0.49 to 1.86%, the worst edge 74 to 308%.
  - **Diagnosed with a throwaway run** (a temporary printout, not kept): at 1 cm, 4% of the edges were over 5%, all in the bodice (1.0-1.5 m up), three quarters of them within 1 cm of the body: the dress hangs from its shoulders through two 7 cm straps. Pinned, the worst were the back neckline's middle, tethered through the neck. The 1 cm dress held by contact at 60 substeps stretched 0.115% on average and 1.7% at the 99th percentile, its hem 5.4 cm higher: XPBD's convergence error, not contact's.
  - **Why the tethers fail on a body:** a tether is a straight line; from a strap to the waist it runs through the bust, shorter than the draped cloth, so it stays slack, and where one is taut it pulls the cloth into the body, which pushes back.
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: the garment measures on a sheet at rest, grown 10%, with one edge pulled (the percentile not seeing it), a seam's gap, a point inside a ball's field; the preparation each tick in order; the dress's hem, a tick's preparation, the dress round a box standing in for the mannequin, pinned and not; the launch option); the release tests pass, the debug game builds without warnings.

- `cheaper-substeps` (Oct 27-28 2026): cheaper substeps on the GPU, then more of them. The dress test showed XPBD's error is convergence (60 substeps cut the 1 cm dress's stretch eightfold), so the question was how to buy substeps.
  - **Explained first; the user chose** cutting each substep's cost before raising the count. A GPU cloth tick costs its dispatches (about 2.4 microseconds each whatever their size), so the levers were fewer colours and fewer passes.
  - **The fewest edge colours (DSatur, Brelaz 1979):** the constraint whose neighbours already use the most different colours is coloured next, ties to the most neighbours then the first, through a queue that skips stale entries, so the result stays deterministic. A grid's edges went to 6 and the dress's to 7, the most edges meeting at one point and so the fewest possible (greedy in the mesh's order gave the dress 9); its hinges 16-18 against 18. The new solving order shut the pinned dress's last stitch 8 ticks later in the lab test, which was loosened to match.
  - **Bending less often** (`bendingInterval`, multiple time stepping as in molecular dynamics): the bending solved every k-th substep with its compliance term divided by k, so a soft constraint gets k substeps' impulse. Tested on a cantilever (within 5% at every 1 and every 4). In the dress sweep, every 4th threw seams open and the hem about at 0.7 cm and finer, and a flat sheet confirmed it; every 2nd held cotton to 0.5 cm but blew denim up there. Stability depends on spacing, fabric and step together, so the interval stays 1, kept as a setting.
  - **Tried and dropped:** fusing the prediction into the last substep's end pass, which raced: the tethers in the end pass read pins the prediction slides.
  - **The sweep** (the 1 cm dress held by contact, GPU, release): 15 substeps 375 dispatches, 0.93 ms a tick, 0.89% mean stretch, 9.7% at the 99th percentile; 30 bending every 2nd about 1.4 ms, 0.31%, 4.4%; 30 every substep 750 dispatches, 1.86 ms, 0.31%, 4.1%; 60 3.9 ms, 0.13%, 1.9%. None reach the 1% mark at the 99th percentile.
  - **The user chose 30 substeps, bending every substep:** a third of the stretch for twice the cost, with no new stability risk. The CPU doubles too (the 1 cm dress about 28 ms a tick, already over budget at 15, so the lab runs it on the GPU).
  - **Floats at 30:** gravity moves a point 0.76 micrometres a substep, and a float 1 m up resolves only 0.06. The hand-spring test, 1 m up, settled 1.65% off (34% at 60), so it moved to the origin (within 0.13%); a free sheet's momentum drifts twice as far as at 15 (0.32% of its speed in half a second), its test's tolerance doubled. One more reason to stop at 30.
  - **A seam frozen open, found by the dress test at 30 substeps:** one armpit stitch of the 2 cm pinned dress stuck at 3.9 mm (10 on the GPU). A probe (a throwaway program) showed it fixed in place from 1.5 s, and with friction lowered or off it shut: the stitch pulls along the chord, which there runs mostly into the body, and static friction undid the slide every substep (at 30 substeps the slides are half as long). **Explained first; the user chose** letting a seam being sewn slide without friction (`markSewing`; on the GPU the end's pass reads each point's partner), over solving stitches after the bodies or leaving it. Tested on two pieces stitched 140 degrees apart round a ball, which fails without the fix and now meets at the top.
  - **Measured at the default** (`make yardstick-dress`): held by contact at 1 cm, 0.30% mean stretch and 4.0% at the 99th percentile (0.96% and 10.5% at 15 substeps), its hem 4 cm higher; about a third at every rung, none passing the 1% mark. The CPU 2.0-2.3 times (31 ms a tick at 1 cm), the GPU 1.6-1.9 ms at any size. Every seam shut. The table is in docs/CLOTHGPU.md, Part 5.
  - **A swaying hem, recorded for the next branch:** the 0.7 cm pinned dress's back hem keeps swinging about 6 cm through 20 s, where it settled without the seam fix. A probe showed it sways with the tethers off too, and every seam is shut in the first second, so the fix only changed the drape; something feeds the swing. The user chose to record it and chase it first in self-collision (the skirt passing through itself is one suspect).
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: the bending interval's substep counts and droop, the GPU's dispatch count, DSatur's colour counts on a grid and the dress, the defaults, sewing's marks, contacts without friction while sewn, the seam round a ball, the GPU's stitch partners); the release tests pass, the debug game, its shaders and the GPU tests build without warnings. Not run: `make gputest` (the bend pass's new push constant, `clothStitchWidest`'s new case, the end's pass sewing without friction).

- `dress-ease` (Oct 28 2026): a roomier dress. The user, in the lab: "the dress is a bit small for the model".
  - **Explained first; the user chose** 8 cm more round the bust: each panel 2 cm wider at each side seam (54 cm across at the armpit, 84 at the hem), 14 cm of ease against 6 (the chest is 94 cm round), a relaxed fit; straps, neckline and length unchanged, so the pins and the hem stay put. The other choices were 4 cm (a fitted 10 cm of ease) and scaling the whole pattern 5% (which would have moved the straps off the shoulders and lengthened the dress).
  - **The straps had to start higher:** held by contact, the wider bodice's side seams, closing, dragged the left strap off the shoulder's end onto the arm's root, where its seam froze 9 cm open (caught by the lab test's seam check). Probed (a throwaway program) with the start 5 cm higher and the wrapping cylinder 2 cm wider, each and together: only the higher start sewed both ways (the wider cylinder let a shoulder seam open and the dress fall to the floor). The straps now start about 15 cm over the shoulders.
  - **The last stitch, pinned:** the one at the armpit waits 3.7 cm open through the weightless sewing (its pull runs straight into the armpit) and shuts 11 ticks after gravity comes on (2.6 cm before); the lab test's limit went from 3 to 4 cm.
  - **Measured** (`make yardstick-dress`, docs/CLOTHGPU.md, Part 5): less stretch at every rung (the 1 cm dress held by contact 0.26% mean and 3.3% at the 99th percentile, against 0.30% and 4.0%; its worst edge 19% against 37%), every seam shut, and the 0.7 cm pinned dress's swaying hem gone (19 mm in its last second, against 74).
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: the pattern at least a quarter of the chest plus 3.5 cm at the armpit, the straps starting 10-20 cm over the shoulders, the lab's dress sewing shut held either way); the release tests pass, the debug game builds without warnings.

- `cloth-self-collision` (Oct 28-29 2026): self-collision on the CPU, as planned on Oct 28 (docs/CLOTHGPU.md, The plan, step 5): contacts between points and triangles and between edges a thickness apart, and Planar-DAT's cut so nothing passes through. The user asked for no slow test runs (simulations are judged live in the game), so the dress test's crossings column and the 0.7 cm hem check wait for the GPU branch.
  - **The paper's lambda checked:** the plane's place as printed (lambda = delta_t / (delta_t + delta_v)) would give a point falling onto a still triangle no room at all; the paper's text says each side's room is in proportion to its own approach, which is followed and tested.
  - **Two holes found by the tests and closed:** skipping every feature one edge from a point let a strip of 5 cm squares crumpling on the floor fold a square through the next (neighbours are now contacts unless already within the thickness at rest); and with the self-contacts before the bodies, as planned, the floor put back the bottom layer's share of every push, so the layers' gap shrank by the cut's 15% margin each window (1.9 mm, 0.8, 0.3, 0.03, 0.002, traced substep by substep) until rounding let an edge through. The self-contacts now come after the bodies, and a point approaching from on its plane stops.
  - **Narrower and more often:** 1 cm every 5 substeps made a 1 cm mesh's flat neighbours (8.7 mm apart) pairs every substep; 7.5 mm every 3 substeps leaves them out, and lifts the speed limit to 3.8 m/s.
  - **Measured** (release, 16 threads): the cloth lab on the CPU with its dress 37 ms a tick, 166-218 ms with self-collision (the user: "super slow, but it seems to work"), nearly all of it finding pairs: the engine's `UniformGrid` gave way to a counting-sort spatial hash, a block of points a job, then mailboxing instead of sorting each query and the distance checked first (15 to 10 ms a search for the dress). The user: "not really that important to get it perfect on the CPU, as we plan to run large garments on the GPU anyway". What is left (two hash builds on one thread, 3.9 million edge candidates) is noted for the GPU port.
  - **The pile sheet,** the cloth lab's self-collision station, the mat's "sheet dropped onto itself" from the layout's plan.
  - **Checked:** the tests (release, tests first: the neighbourhood, flat sheets left alone and a fold over the next square found, the plane and the cut against the paper's equations, rounding past a plane, the cap, pinned points, contacts and friction, the crossings counter, two stacked sheets (falling through without), the strip piling on the floor, determinism, the settings reachable by key, the lab's pile, barycentric weights and segment shares); the release tests pass, the debug game builds without warnings. The user saw it work in the lab on the CPU.
